# Premium colour palettes and surface/material treatments 2025–2026 (warm, feminine, calm ADHD command-centre PWA)

> Method note: research done 2026-11-26 using web search only. Every direct page fetch (pantone.com, joshwcomeau.com, 925studios.co) failed because the session's network allowlist blocks those hosts, so the findings below rest on search-result snippets and summaries, not full pages. Hex values from third-party sites are marked as such. Awwwards/Godly/Siteinspire/Mobbin pages could not be browsed in detail, so studio/site references are thin (see Gaps).

## 1. Which colour directions are current (trend reports + curation) that feel warm, feminine and luxurious without cliché?

### Takeaway
The 2025–2026 signals all point toward **warm neutrals as the base, with one deep "jewel/food" colour as the anchor**: Mocha Mousse (2025) gave way to Cloud Dancer, a soft warm white (2026). Brown (chocolate/cocoa), deep red/burgundy/plum and muted green (jade/sage) are rising. Butter yellow peaked in 2025 and now works best as small accents paired with burgundy. The current cream + terracotta look is on-trend. The way to make it premium is to deepen the darks (cocoa, plum noir, burgundy) and mute the pastels, not to add more colours.

### Inferences
These three directions are my own synthesis. The hex values not attributed above are proposals, not sourced.
- **A "Mocha & Cloud" (quiet luxury, editorial):** Cloud Dancer #F0EEE9 or Wax-Paper-like warm white as the base, Mocha Mousse #A47864 and a cocoa dark (around #3B2A24, proposal) for text and anchors, with terracotta reduced to a single accent. Feels like a hotel or skincare brand. It is the closest step from today's cream/terracotta look.
- **B "Cherry & Butter" (feminine, confident):** off-white base, Berry Red #941E2C or burgundy as the single action colour, butter #F9E8A8 only for highlights and "done" moments, plum noir #351E28 for text. Trend-backed by the Lick and Davey & Krista pairing. Keep butter yellow small, since it is past its peak.
- **C "Jade & Plum" (calm, cool-warm balance):** warm white, Jade #AEB8A0 or sage #C4D4B8 for area tints, Plum Noir #351E28 as the dark, and Persimmon #FF5C34 or terracotta used very sparingly as the one alert colour (Pinterest's own pairing). Calmest option for ADHD, and less "brown".
- Across all three, the premium feel comes from a **near-black that isn't black** (cocoa or plum) and **muted, greyed pastels** in place of candy pastels.

## 2. Which material/surface treatments are in use, and how are they built on the web?

### Takeaway
Depth and tactility are back in a restrained form: grain/paper noise, layered tinted shadows, light "neo-skeuomorphic" relief and Apple's Liquid Glass (WWDC 2025). On the web, grain is cheap and robust (an SVG `feTurbulence` overlay). True Liquid Glass refraction (`backdrop-filter: url(#svg)`) is effectively Chromium-only and carries legibility risks, so plain frosted `blur()`+`saturate()` is the realistic fallback.

### Cited Findings
**Apple Liquid Glass (WWDC 2025, iOS 26)**
- Apple calls it "a new digital meta-material that dynamically bends and shapes light". Shadows and tints shift with the content underneath. It thickens when a menu expands. The HIG says apps should keep a clear hierarchy between content and controls. — [WWDC25 session 219 "Meet Liquid Glass"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/); [Design Milk](https://design-milk.com/apple-returns-to-a-layered-software-design-with-liquid-glass-interface/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_Glass)
- Wikipedia mentions a Liquid Glass revamp announced at WWDC 2026 (iOS 27). Not verified. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_Glass)

**Liquid Glass on the web**
- Common technique: an SVG filter chain (blur → `feDisplacementMap`, with the map from `feImage` or `feTurbulence`) referenced via `backdrop-filter: url(#filter)`. This works only in Chromium; Safari ignores it. Gate it with `@supports` and fall back to `blur()` + `saturate()`. A Chromium race can flash the surface transparent if the SVG node isn't in the DOM yet. — [Frontend Masters "Liquid Glass on the Web"](https://frontendmasters.com/blog/?p=6644); [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/how-create-liquid-glass-effects-css-and-svg/); [Ekino/Medium, Jul 2025](https://medium.com/ekino-france/liquid-glass-in-css-and-svg-839985fcb88d); [DEV: 6 ways](https://dev.to/devyatov/liquid-glass-on-the-web-6-ways-to-build-it-with-css-and-svg-3m07); [Tuts+](https://webdesign.tutsplus.com/liquid-glass-effect-svg-filters--cms-109200t)

**Grain / paper noise**
- An SVG `feTurbulence` filter generates Perlin noise, used as a background image or an overlay. Frontend Masters' "Grainy Gradients" uses `baseFrequency≈0.97`, `numOctaves=4` and `color-interpolation-filters='sRGB'`. Other techniques: layer the noise over a CSS gradient and tune it with `contrast()`/`brightness()`, blend with `mix-blend-mode: overlay|multiply`, embed it as a data-URI to save a request, or put it on a fixed low-opacity pseudo-element and reduce it in dark mode. — [Frontend Masters "Grainy Gradients"](https://frontendmasters.com/blog/grainy-gradients/); [CSS-Tricks](https://css-tricks.com/?p=351302); [freeCodeCamp](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/grainy-css-backgrounds-using-svg-filters/); [Codrops (2019, older) – feTurbulence + lighting for paper texture](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2019/02/19/svg-filter-effects-creating-texture-with-feturbulence/); [CodyHouse noise component](https://codyhouse.co/ds/components/info/noise-background)

**Layered, tinted shadows (the "soft object" look)**
- Josh W. Comeau's "Designing Beautiful Shadows in CSS" (2021, updated Apr 2026) builds on Tobias Ahlin's layered shadows: stack several shadows with increasing offset and blur and lower alpha on each, keep one consistent light source, and tint shadows instead of using grey. His Shadow Palette Generator outputs elevation tokens (`--shadow-elevation-low/medium/high`). — [Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/designing-shadows); [Shadow Palette Generator](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/css/introducing-shadow-palette-generator/); [Tobias Ahlin](https://tobiasahlin.com/blog/layered-smooth-box-shadows/); [CSS-Tricks summary](https://css-tricks.com/?p=358926)

**Neo-skeuomorphism / clay / tactile**
- The 2025–26 version is "restrained": selective tactile cues on flat layouts. Classic neumorphism is now "a tactic rather than a trend" and often fails contrast. Claymorphism is listed as fitting "friendly products" and onboarding. Some argue Liquid Glass is "skeuomorphism in disguise". These sources are mostly vendor blogs. — [daisyUI material/dimensional trends](https://trends.daisyui.com/material-dimensional-ui/); [daisyUI 18 trends 2026](https://trends.daisyui.com/ui-design-trends-2026/); [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/best-designs/websites/trends/neumorphism-website); [Webdesigner Depot](https://webdesignerdepot.com/how-apple-quietly-brought-skeuomorphism-back-to-life/); [Creative Bloq – retro icons, Apple to Airbnb](https://www.creativebloq.com/web-design/ux-ui/from-apple-to-airbnb-retro-icon-design-is-making-a-comeback)

### Inferences
- Material pairing per direction. **A Mocha & Cloud:** paper grain (opacity 3–6%) plus warm brown-tinted layered shadows, like letterpress or uncoated stock. **B Cherry & Butter:** enamel/porcelain, meaning a glossy top highlight (a 1px inset light `box-shadow`) plus deep tinted shadow on the active card, with matte surroundings. **C Jade & Plum:** frosted acrylic, a `backdrop-filter: blur(16–24px) saturate(1.2)` panel used **only** for the top bar and sheets, never behind body text.
- For a swipeable card stack, layered shadows plus a slight scale/offset of the cards beneath are the cheapest way to get "physical object" depth. Grain on the cards themselves (not just the background) reads more "paper".
- Avoid the SVG-displacement Liquid Glass: it is Chromium-only, and the PWA will often run in iOS Safari.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for risograph, fabric or embossed-UI implementations in 2025–26 product UI. Rauno Freiberg, Emil Kowalski, Vercel, Linear and Family blog material posts were not retrieved.

## 3. What makes palettes look "AI slop", and how to avoid it?

### Takeaway
The recognised fingerprint is an indigo/violet gradient (especially `#667eea → #764ba2`), Inter, rounded cards with soft grey shadows, a 3-up icon grid and generic glassmorphism and glows. It comes from models and templates converging on defaults such as Tailwind's indigo-500. The fix is deliberate, constrained choices: a named palette with a specific dark, one accent, tinted neutrals and a real material idea.

### Cited Findings
- Common bundle: Inter, rounded cards with subtle shadows, three icon feature boxes, purple-to-blue hero gradient. Models drift to the "statistical center of 'nice modern web UI'". — [superdesign.dev](https://superdesign.dev/blog/why-ai-design-looks-generic); [DEV: blame Tailwind's indigo-500](https://dev.to/alanwest/why-every-ai-built-website-looks-the-same-blame-tailwinds-indigo-500-3h2p); [DEV: AI purple problem](https://dev.to/jaainil/ai-purple-problem-make-your-ui-unmistakable-3ono)
- Specific "AI default purple gradient": `linear-gradient(135deg, #667eea 0%, #764ba2 100%)`. — [ux-skill wiki: Anti AI slop ban list](https://github.com/Laith0003/ux-skill/wiki/Anti-AI-slop-ban-list); [How to detect AI slop](https://github.com/Laith0003/ux-skill/wiki/How-to-detect-AI-slop-in-your-design)
- Fixes: choose a specific typeface, describe the layout yourself, reference a design system and give brand constraints. These patterns do not prove AI use ("before AI, all websites looked like Bootstrap"). — [DEV: fix the AI-generated look](https://dev.to/alanwest/how-to-fix-the-ai-generated-look-in-your-frontend-1ahh); [Kosta Canatselis – Spot the slop](https://world.hey.com/kostac/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designer-s-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults-4c448c9c); [Gigazine Apr 2026 on "design slop"](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260423-design-slop); [925studios – AI slop fonts and gradients](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells) (page not fetchable)

### Inferences
- For this app, the "AI" risks are pastel area tints that are fully saturated and evenly spaced around the hue wheel, grey `rgba(0,0,0,.1)` shadows, glass panels with neon edges, and gradient blobs. Counter-moves: derive the area tints from one or two hue families (e.g. all slightly brown-greyed), tint shadows with the dark brand colour, use a characterful serif or grotesk for headings, and pick one material per design.
- Trend authority (Pantone, WGSN, Pinterest hex values) gives a defensible, non-default origin for each palette.

### Gaps
- The sources are blogs and community wikis, not authoritative studies. No statements from named design leads (Rauno, Emil Kowalski, Linear) were retrieved.

## 4. Colour accessibility for an ADHD audience

### Takeaway
There is no peer-reviewed evidence that muted palettes help ADHD users specifically. The guidance consensus (W3C COGA, UK gov, UX practitioners) is: minimise distraction (motion and competing accents matter more than hue), keep high text contrast, avoid highly saturated or fluorescent fields, use colour meaningfully (urgency, readability) and let users customise. Liquid Glass-style transparency is a known legibility risk.

### Cited Findings
- W3C COGA "Making Content Usable" (W3C Note 2021) advises avoiding distraction from the task and offering headings and breadcrumbs to regain context. It is supplementary to WCAG and not required for conformance. — [W3C COGA Note 2021](https://www.w3.org/TR/2021/NOTE-coga-usable-20210429/)
- UK Dept. for Education COGA summary: animations, banners and adverts "can be extremely distracting… especially problematic for people with ADHD". Guideline 4 includes "use colour to enhance readability and urgency". — [UK DfE COGA guidelines](https://accessibility.education.gov.uk/guidelines/coga); [UK DfE knowledge hub](https://accessibility.education.gov.uk/knowledge-hub/coga)
- Neurodivergent UX guidance: highly saturated schemes are a problem for light-sensitive users; consider muted, low-stimulation colours and customisable themes. — [Print Mag – Designing for the 15%](https://www.printmag.com/industry-perspectives/why-neurodivergent-ux-is-the-future/); [accessibilitychecker.org](https://accessibilitychecker.org/blog/neurodivergent-ux-design); [Univ. of St Andrews – Designing for users with ADHD (Feb 2025)](https://digitalcommunications.wp.st-andrews.ac.uk/2025/02/12/designing-for-users-with-adhd/)
- Claim that bright, fluorescent colours raise arousal and impair sustained attention while soft blues and greens are calmer. The source admits individual variation, and the underlying studies were not found. Treat as weak evidence. — [NeuroLaunch](https://neurolaunch.com/adhd-colours-to-avoid/)
- Liquid Glass critique: weak figure-ground contrast; automated tests can't evaluate complex transparency; "Reduce Transparency" mitigates it. Jakob Nielsen says low-vision users are most exposed. — [Fast Company](https://fastcompanyme.com/?p=41660); [Infinum](https://infinum.com/blog/apples-ios-26-liquid-glass-sleek-shiny-and-questionably-accessible/); [WebProNews (secondhand NN/g)](https://www.webpronews.com/apples-ios-26-liquid-glass-design-draws-usability-critique-from-experts)
- Classic neumorphism's low-contrast controls often fail accessibility checks. — [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/best-designs/websites/trends/neumorphism-website)
- Tiimo (App of the Year 2025) combines soft colours with colour as a cue, plus user-chosen themes and fonts. A real-world validation of the "soft but meaningful" approach. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1480220328)

### Inferences
- Practical rules for all three designs: body text ≥ 4.5:1 (WCAG 1.4.3) on cream; meaningful UI edges/icons ≥ 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11); **one action accent + one alert colour max**; area tints only as low-chroma backgrounds and never as the only signal (pair with icon/label); honour `prefers-reduced-transparency` / `prefers-contrast` and `prefers-reduced-motion`; grain ≤ ~6% opacity, off in high-contrast mode.
- Terracotta #c8553d on cream #f4ece2 is likely around 3.5–4:1 (my estimate, not measured), fine for large text and icons but marginal for small text. Verify with a checker.

### Gaps
- No controlled study (CHI etc.) comparing muted vs saturated palettes for ADHD users was found.
- WCAG ratio values cited from general knowledge; the W3C pages were not fetched in this session.
